A booking, step by step

What happens when someone books a call

  1. The visitor asks for an intro call. The assistant calls check_availability, which reads the calendar and returns real free slots in the visitor's own time zone.
  2. It collects name, email, company and topic one question at a time, then reads the details back and asks “Shall I go ahead?”
  3. Only after a clear yes does it call book_intro_call. n8n re-checks the slot, creates the calendar event and verifies it, so two people can't take the same time.
  4. n8n saves the meeting to the CRM records, alerts the right team by email and prepares the visitor's confirmation email.
  5. The assistant reports the booking and its reference only after the workflow confirms it. If anything fails, it says so and offers a person.
  6. When the conversation ends, Retell sends the transcript, summary and measured cost to the dashboard.

Guardrails

Built-in limits

Clear AI disclosure

Every chat and call opens by saying it's an AI. Voice calls ask for recording consent and end if the caller declines.

Confirm before acting

Bookings, saved enquiries, emails and handovers all need an explicit yes, and the workflows reject requests that weren't confirmed.

Approved knowledge only

The assistant answers from approved content and says “I don't have that” rather than guessing. Prices are guides, never quotes.

Human handover

On request, on complaints, or when it can't resolve something, it creates a ticket for the right team with a reply time.

Data care

No passwords, card numbers or health data. Transcripts are kept 30 days. Webhooks are authenticated and the dashboard needs an access key.

Transparent costs

Each conversation's provider cost is recorded and shown in the dashboard, so running costs are measured rather than estimated.
